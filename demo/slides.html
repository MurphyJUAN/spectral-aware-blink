The problem

A perfectly constant star looks 2× variable in SPHEREx — because each exposure sees it in a different colour

Left: brightness vs time — looks like a variable star.
Middle: the same points vs wavelength — a smooth spectrum.
Right: inside a survey pass, wavelength sweeps with time. Blinking images to find Planet X or brown dwarfs is blinking a rainbow.
SPHEREx linear variable filter, 102 channels, 0.75–5 µm, 6.15″ pixels2 / 7
What we built

Learn each source's spectrum from the other passes, subtract it, and blink only what is left

01 · DATA
Public NASA files only
SPHEREx DR1 cutouts by HTTP range-read from the IRSA AWS mirror; per-pixel wavelength from the FITS headers. 258 exposures, 10 fields.
02 · MODEL
Spectrum per source
Smooth spectrum per detector, fitted leave-one-pass-out so a moving or varying object cannot hide in its own model.
03 · RESIDUAL
Blink the difference
Motion → +/− dipole. Variability → coherent residual. Colour flicker → gone.
04 · TEST
Fair benchmark
Naive blink, same-colour pairs, detector stacks vs ours; same pipeline, equal false-alarm rate, injected fakes.

The colour sweep invents variable stars — and we can predict exactly how much from metadata alone

Why it matters for Planet X hunters

The objects we most want to find — cold brown dwarfs — are the ones colour-blind methods fake the most

1.3–2.4×
cleaner with our model for the most colour-peculiar quarter of real stars (9 of 10 fields)
0 extra data
needed to predict it: pass wavelengths from headers + one colour per source
Relative-colour law: after the usual zero-point match, fake variability ∝ (source colour − field colour) × colour shift between passes. Confirmed in 10/10 fields (r = 0.58–0.89).

Validation — including what did not work

The coldest known brown dwarf, WISE 0855, shows up as a moving dipole that flips sign over the year

≈ 8σ
combined signal in 3.9–5 µm residuals (3–5σ per pass, measured at its predicted position); nothing at short wavelengths — exactly a Y dwarf's fingerprint.
1,920
fake moving sources injected into real images and scored at equal false-alarm rate. Naive blink is 2–5× shallower than any colour-aware method.
HONEST NEGATIVE RESULT
At the single-pixel level SPHEREx's PSF is undersampled (1.2 px), and that — not colour — limits blinking for star-like colours. For brown-dwarf colours colour-blind stacks still lose ≥1.6×. Next step: PSF forward modelling.

Blink without false alarms
Backyard-Worlds-style blinking on SPHEREx images, with colour flicker removed and a per-star "will it fake-blink?" warning.
SPHEREX TIME DOMAIN
Trustworthy variability
Planned survey-to-survey variability flags collapse faint sources into ~10 bands; our law predicts they will over-flag colour-peculiar sources unless spectrum-aware.
PLANET X / BROWN DWARFS
Find the coldest movers
Y/T dwarfs are where colour-blind methods fail by 26–36%; spectrum-aware residuals keep them at the ~3% floor.
